# Research and usability evidence

Research validates problems and task outcomes; it does not justify a preferred look. Contents: plan, methods, evidence rules, privacy, reporting.

## Plan

Decision to inform, hypotheses, audience, representative tasks with a success criterion each, recruitment criteria, consent and privacy handling, measures (task success, time on task, errors, assists, abandonment, satisfaction), and the validation that would follow.

## Methods

- Moderated sessions for discovery and complex flows; unmoderated for narrow tasks at scale. Five to eight participants per distinct user group is an industry rule of thumb (unverified here against a primary source) for surfacing recurring problems; it supports no statistical claim.
- Include keyboard-only and screen-reader users, zoom/low-vision users and the supported languages and devices when the product serves them.
- Use realistic content and data; empty prototypes hide layout and wording problems.
- Heuristic review and analytics are complements, not substitutes for observing tasks.

## Evidence rules

- Separate observation ("3 of 6 missed the Save button below the fold at 320 px") from interpretation.
- Record sample size, recruitment bias, environment, incomplete sessions and generalization limits.
- A before/after screenshot or an internal opinion is not evidence of improved usability or preference. State only what was observed; mark user-outcome gains "not measured" otherwise.
- Convert findings to prioritized issues: context to reproduce, severity (blocks task, slows task, cosmetic), confidence, proposed change, validation plan.

## Privacy

Collect the minimum personal data; consent for recording and reuse; remove names and identifiers from notes; define retention and deletion; keep recordings access-controlled. Do not store session recordings from production forms that may contain personal data without a documented lawful basis.

## Output

Plan or findings table plus explicit "not tested" list. A review without sessions delivers heuristics and hypotheses only.
